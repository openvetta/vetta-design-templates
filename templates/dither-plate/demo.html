01 — Registers

Three sheets, three pictures. Sheet 14 is paper and a short palette: pale, mid, deep, and ink, cells at 10px. Sheet 07 is the same paper, cells at 6px, and two flat plates in the accent blue. The night sheet is pale gray and one ink, cells at 8px. A sheet does not borrow another sheet's ground.

02 — The cell

The matrix is 4 by 4. A cell is on when its Bayer value sits under the threshold for that ink. Inks in one tile take different values, so two colors never share a square and never mix. The square does not grow. Empty cells are the ground. Nothing is blurred and nothing is set to a partial opacity.

Depth on these sheets is overlap of opaque shapes: a plate behind a crystal, a plate across a crystal, a wing painted over a tail. The shadow tokens are none.

03 — Type and controls

Vespera is the sliced display face. Vein is the sharp serif, with an italic stack under it. Kiln is drawn blackletter, and its N is the dither grid. Captions, the mast, and the night meta are mono. The press room's controls use the mono, square, with no radius and no shadow.

  • Stack. Below 800px each sheet is one column. Sheet 14 puts the sliced name above the moth and the captions under it, left aligned. The wide sheet's side-by-side lockup is gone, so the wing does not cover the name.
  • Drawings. The moth, the crystal, and the beast go to the full column width and keep their aspect ratio. They are not cropped. Plates stay inside the crystal drawing, so they cannot cover the word Vein.
  • Night sheet. Meta wraps. Then the lockup, the beast, and the footer. Pale gray still runs edge to edge.
  • Type. The sliced name floors at 52px on the sheet and is 40px inside this frame. Vein floors at 72px. Mono captions stay 12 to 13px. Guide body stays 17px.
  • Targets. Buttons and the edition field grow to 48px. Cells, plates, and eyespots are not targets. A coarse pointer has no hover-only state. The solid button's resting fill is the state.
  • Width. Padding is 16px. Captions wrap inside it. Nothing scrolls sideways.
